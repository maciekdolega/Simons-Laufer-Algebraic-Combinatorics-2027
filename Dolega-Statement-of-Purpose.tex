\smallskip
 
My research lies at the intersection of integrable probability, enumerative geometry and mathematical physics, and what connects these areas in my work is algebraic and enumerative combinatorics. More specifically, I study the combinatorics of Jack and Macdonald symmetric functions and how their hidden combinatorial structure explains phenomena in these fields. Each of my current projects needs exactly the mix of communities that the Fall~2027 semester brings to Berkeley: algebraic combinatorialists working on symmetric functions and integrability and, through the concurrent program \emph{New Trends in Tropical Geometry}, tropical and real enumerative geometers. I plan to use my stay to advance the following four projects.
 
\proj{A. $b$-Hurwitz numbers: towards a geometric theory.}
In~[J8]\footnote{References [J$\ast$], [E$\ast$] and [F$\ast$] refer to my list of publications attached to this application.} we introduced \emph{$b$-Hurwitz numbers}, a deformation of Hurwitz numbers by a parameter $b$ that measures non-orientability. Their generating function is a partition function built from Jack symmetric functions, the one-parameter deformation of Schur functions, which are recovered at $b=0$. $b$-Hurwitz numbers count possibly non-orientable branched coverings of the sphere with weight $b^{\nu}$, where $\nu>0$ if and only if the covering surface is non-orientable. The model thus interpolates between classical complex Hurwitz numbers ($b=0$) and their real counterparts ($b=1$), and places Jack symmetric functions within real enumerative geometry for the first time. With collaborators I showed that $b$-Hurwitz numbers are computed by refined topological recursion~[J1] and arise from Whittaker vectors for $\mathcal{W}$-algebras~[E3]; in special cases they are partition functions of matrix models, such as the BGW integral, and satisfy the BKP hierarchy~[J5]. Tropical geometers have taken up the theory: Hahn--Markwig found tropical and polynomial structures at $b=1$ (\emph{twisted Hurwitz numbers}), and Fesler--Hahn--Karev--Markwig used tropicalization to answer our question on the polynomiality of simple $b$-Hurwitz numbers.
 
The central open problem I want to attack is geometric: \emph{what is the geometric meaning of the full multiparametric Jack generating series?} Already the case of three sets of variables, which for $b=0$ enumerates Grothendieck's dessins d'enfants, is the \emph{$b$-conjecture} of Goulden and Jackson (1996), still unresolved. My earlier work settled special cases of it~[J17,~J18,~J20] and led to our study of the closely related two-point multiparametric Jack expansions~[J8], where $b$-Hurwitz numbers were born. Our work in progress suggests that the key is to relate the combinatorics of certain integrable operators to the tropical side of real enumerative geometry. The joint workshops of the two programs and daily contact with the tropical and real enumerative geometers of the concurrent program, in particular H.~Markwig and her collaborators, make the semester the ideal setting for this question.
 
\proj{B. Positivity for Jack polynomials.}
Another long-standing conjecture that I find particularly beautiful is due to Lassalle (2009). Motivated by the asymptotic representation theory of symmetric groups and free probability, he conjectured that \emph{Jack characters}, the normalized coefficients of Jack polynomials in the power-sum basis, are expressed in terms of free cumulants by universal \emph{Kerov polynomials} with non-negative integer coefficients. With H.~Ben~Dali I found a positive formula for Jack polynomials and Jack characters and proved two conjectures of Lassalle, including the integrality of Kerov polynomials~[E4]; their positivity remains open. Our approach merges two combinatorial viewpoints: maps, the objects underlying $b$-Hurwitz theory, and operators on symmetric functions, which we understand combinatorially through lattice paths. All these operators come from the representation of the Schiffmann--Vasserot algebra $\mathrm{SH}^c$ on symmetric functions. The new tool I want to develop is a general combinatorial interpretation of this representation in the spirit of~[E4,~E1], which should give a structural route to Kerov positivity. Symmetric functions, integrability and combinatorial representation theory are core themes of the program, and its concentration of experts on Macdonald and Jack combinatorics is what this project needs.
 
\proj{C. Discrete $\beta$-ensembles at high temperature.}
Discrete $N$-particle systems with Jack-type interaction are discrete counterparts of $\beta$-ensembles and central objects of integrable probability. For fixed $\beta$, their large-$N$ behaviour, properly scaled, does not depend on $\beta$, just as for continuous $\beta$-ensembles. The high-temperature regime $\beta\to0$, $\beta N\to\mathrm{const}$ is very different and was not understood until recently. With C.~Cuenca I characterized all such systems that have a limit shape in this regime~[J2]. Here a probabilistic problem is solved almost entirely by algebraic combinatorics: the action of Cherednik--Dunkl operators on symmetric functions gives the moments of the empirical measure, which we express as weighted sums of lattice paths. Two questions follow: what is the shape of the limit, and what are the fluctuations around it? For the first, we discovered a \emph{crystallization} phenomenon with no counterpart for continuous $\beta$-ensembles~[E2]: in three cases the limit density equals one on intervals separated by gaps of length one, so it is determined by the right endpoints of these intervals, the \emph{crystals}. We proved that certain classical hypergeometric functions are real-rooted and that the crystals are exactly their zeros. We believe this is a general phenomenon, and we plan to prove it for the whole class by locating the crystals as the real zeros of a ``convolution'' of real-rooted entire functions. For the fluctuations, the challenge is to control the subleading terms of the mixed moments, not only the leading one. Lattice paths, which already control the leading order of the moments in~[J2] and also appear in project~B, should be powerful enough to capture the whole asymptotic expansion. This is far from straightforward, however: it requires new combinatorial ideas, such as suitable statistics on lattice paths that single out the classes of paths contributing to the subleading terms. The project lies where two focus areas of the program meet, \emph{integrable systems and dynamical combinatorics} and \emph{geometry of polynomials}. Discussions with S.~Corteel, Ph.~Di~Francesco and L.~Williams (integrable combinatorics, combinatorial moment problem, symmetric functions, asymptotic enumeration) and with C.~Vinzant (real-rootedness) would be particularly valuable.
 
\proj{D. General-$\beta$ matrix integrals, with J.~Novak.}
With J.~Novak (UCSD) I study the large-$N$ asymptotics of the general-$\beta$ analogues of the Harish-Chandra--Itzykson--Zuber and Brézin--Gross--Witten integrals. These are multivariate Bessel functions; the second also appears in gauge theory as a Gaiotto state. Novak's approach for $\beta=2$ relies on monotone Hurwitz numbers and their combinatorics. The missing general-$\beta$ ingredients come from my recent work: $b$-monotone Hurwitz numbers with their cut-and-join equation and Virasoro constraints~[J5,~J8], and the asymptotics of Jack--Plancherel diagrams~[J14,~J3]. We have proved a version of the conjectured asymptotics and are writing the paper; the more ambitious general statement remains open. Novak plans to take part in the program, and since we otherwise have little opportunity to work together in person, overlapping at SLMath may be decisive for completing the project.
 
\proj{Contribution to the program.}
I would be happy to give a talk or a short tutorial on any of these topics, and to run a working group on Jack and $b$-deformations across combinatorics, integrability and real and tropical enumerative geometry, as I have done at IM~PAN and at the Jagiellonian University. I have mentored eight postdocs and co-supervised four PhD students, and would gladly mentor postdocs and students of the program. I am also the main organizer of the Simons Semester \href{https://integrable.impan.pl}{\emph{Combinatorial structures, integrable probability, and topological recursion}} at IM~PAN in spring~2027; I will bring the questions and collaborations emerging from it to Berkeley and help strengthen the links between SLMath and the Polish combinatorics community. I plan to be in residence for the first half of the program, covering the Pathways and Introductory Workshops shared with the tropical program and \emph{Recent Developments in Algebraic Combinatorics}. My wife, Jacinta Torres, who works in combinatorial representation theory, is also applying to this program; we plan to come to Berkeley together and both look forward to contributing to the semester.
